OpenWings Daily Privacy Policy

OpenWings Daily is a wellness companion app provided by OpenWings Wellness. It works fully offline and has no accounts, advertising, analytics or cloud sync. This page explains what stays on your device, the few requests the app can make and when, and how reminders and sharing work.

Information on your device

The app stores, in its private local storage: your self-reported mood by date; the optional one-line notes and evening reflections you choose to write; checked daily habits; completed breathing session counts; your chosen breathing pattern; completed yoga and meditation practices and phone-down breaks (counts only); what you choose to keep from "Speak your mind"; the reflection cards you choose to keep; the times of day you finished a practice; saved activities; activities you have tried; journey progress; milestones you have reached; which suggestion Today showed you; any plan you make for tomorrow; and your preferences (reduced motion, sound and its volume, the narrator voice, spoken cues, voice guidance, quiet mode, assistant on or off, gentle nudges, plan reminders, your speech language and whether speech is recognised offline, on the phone's service or by a downloaded model, Wi-Fi-only downloads, and the fresh-content setting). Your garden and streak are calculated from this history. Reminder times are stored by the Android app on the device as well.

We do not collect any of this. It is never transmitted to OpenWings Wellness or to third parties. We do not ask for your name, email, location, contacts or health records.

The one network request: fresh content

From version 2.0 the app can download a small content file (new activities, quotes, journeys and seasonal notes) from content.openwingswellness.com, at most a few times a day and only when you have the "Fresh content from OpenWings" preference turned on (it is on by default). The request carries no account, identifier, or information about you or your use of the app. Like any web request it does reveal your device's IP address to our server, which our hosting provider may log for security and capacity purposes in the normal way. You can turn the preference off in Preferences & privacy; the bundled content keeps working without it. Apart from this, the only other addresses the app can reach are the public download sources for the optional on-device models described below (huggingface.co, its hf.co download servers and cdn.jsdelivr.net), and only while a download you started is running. Tapping our website address opens it in your own browser, outside the app. The Android app blocks the in-app pages from reaching any other address.

Reminders

If you turn on a daily or evening reminder, the Android app schedules a local alarm on your device and shows a notification at the time you chose. This needs Android's notification permission, which the app asks for only when you turn a reminder on. Reminders are processed entirely on your device; nothing about them leaves it.

Sharing

Sharing is always started by you. A share card is drawn on your device and handed to the app you pick in Android's share sheet. A progress card carries your streak, session count, the day's quote and our logo. A reflection card or a weekly card carries the words you chose to keep, and your mood only if you ask for it to be shown; you see the card before you share it, and nothing else from your history is ever on it. Exporting your lines as text also goes only to the app you choose. Once shared, the receiving app's own privacy terms apply.

About you (optional)

The app may ask for your first name, your gender, your age band (never an exact age) and whether your days are mostly spent studying or working at a computer. Each is optional and can be left blank, set to "Prefer not to say", edited or cleared in Preferences at any time. They are stored on your device only and never sent to us. The app uses them to greet you by name, to keep practice suggestions gentle for your age, to add a line about talking to a trusted adult in its support text if you are under 18, to offer activities written for study days or screen days, and to show content OpenWings has written for a particular group. Clearing your data removes them.

Your words

Today shows a few of your own recent words, taken only from the lines, kept thoughts and reflections you chose to save on this device. Nothing is sent; it is your own writing reflected back.

Optional on-device models

Under Preferences, Downloads, you can fetch two optional models: speech to text and a small writing helper. They are plain files, fetched at fixed versions from their public sources, Hugging Face (huggingface.co and its hf.co download servers) and jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net), and kept on your device until you delete them. Downloading one reveals your IP address to those services under their own privacy terms; the app sends them nothing about you, and nothing is downloaded unless you tap Download. With the speech model, hold-to-talk records audio only while you hold the button and turns it into text on your device. The writing helper only suggests text you see first; nothing it writes is saved unless you keep it.

Time and your screen

The app counts the minutes you spend in OpenWings Daily itself, on your device, so it can show you the time you gave back. It does not read which other apps you use, how long you use them, or anything else about your phone, and it asks for no permission to do any of that. In the evening reflection you may optionally answer one question about how your screen felt that day, and optionally type an hours figure yourself. Both are yours to skip, and both stay on the device.

What has been happening

The patterns shown on your You page are worked out on your device from what you have recorded in this app: minutes practised, breaks taken, the times you came by, and your own answer about how your screen felt. Nothing is read from any other app, nothing is sent anywhere, and the app never claims that one thing caused another.

Reflection cards and gentle nudges

When you tap Keep after speaking or writing, the app drafts a reflection card on your device: your words, the time, a short pre-written line and one or two practices chosen by matching words. You decide whether to keep the card, keep only your words, or discard everything; nothing is stored until then. Cards stay on your device and are shared only when you choose. Gentle nudges are worked out on your device from what you did that day, at most one a day, and use the notification permission you already gave.

Planning tomorrow

In the evening the app can offer a few small moments for the next day, worked out on your device from how today actually went. You edit the list, add your own moments, or ignore it. The plan is stored on your device with the rest of your history. If you keep it, the Android app sets local alarms so it can remind you at the times you chose, and it may ask in the morning whether you would like the day armed; nothing is scheduled until you agree. The reminder text is built on your device from the plan, which can include a few words from something you wrote. None of it leaves the phone, and you can turn plan reminders off in Preferences.

Reading your line, on the phone

When you save a line, the app compares your words with a small built-in word list on your device to offer a mood, which you can accept or dismiss. It also remembers which suggestions you finish so it can order them better. Thoughts kept on the Speak your mind page are never read. Nothing about this leaves the device.

Suggestions

The "For you now" card is chosen by a short list of fixed rules that run on your phone: how you said you feel today and over the last few days, the words in your own line, an open journey, the time of day. It shows the reason it used. No model, no profile and no data leaves the device.

Speak your mind

Speak your mind is a blank page: nothing you type or say there is analysed, and nothing is stored unless you tap Keep. Let it go discards the text completely. If you use the microphone there, your device's own speech service turns each sentence into text, as described below.

On-device assistant and voice input

The "Say what you need" box on Today is powered by a small open-source model (Needle 3 by Cactus Compute) that is packaged inside the app and runs on your device. What you type is processed on your phone and is not sent anywhere; the model has no network access. It can only trigger actions you could tap yourself (log a mood, start a practice, open an activity, save a line, set a reminder, start a phone-down break, open the blank page, open your week, or change quiet mode, the soundscape or the narrator), it asks before saving a line or changing a setting, and it does not give advice. If you tap "Report a wrong action", the app prepares a short text with what you said and what it chose; it is only sent if you choose to share it, for example by email to us.

The large "Speak your mind" circle on Today listens only while you hold it. For this the app asks for the microphone permission the first time you hold the button. The audio goes straight to your device's speech recogniser (on most phones provided by Google), which turns it into text under its provider's terms and may use its servers unless offline recognition is available. The app never records, stores or sends audio, and the text is discarded unless you tap Keep. If you use the small microphone button in the assistant box, the app asks the same speech recogniser through a standard Android screen and only receives the finished text. When the downloaded speech model is used, the microphone is opened by the browser component the app is built on, so the app also declares Android's "modify audio settings" permission. Android grants it at install time without asking you, it gives the app no access beyond the microphone permission you have already granted, and it still records and sends nothing. Guided meditations and yoga cues are spoken from audio recordings bundled with the app; nothing is generated on your device. Only a line that has no recording falls back to your device's own text-to-speech voice, which is sent just that line. You can turn the voice off in the player or in Preferences. While a practice runs the app keeps the screen on; nothing else changes. By default the recogniser is asked to work offline, in the language you chose in Preferences (Indian English unless you pick another). If you turn on "Better accuracy online" in Preferences, your phone's speech service may send the audio to its provider for recognition under that provider's terms; the app itself still never records, stores or sends audio.

Sleep mode

A guided meditation can keep playing while the screen is off. For this the app runs a short foreground session on your device with a notification you can pause or stop, and keeps the processor awake only for the length of the meditation. Nothing about the session leaves the device.

Phone-down break

A break schedules one local alarm on your device and, if you ask for it, one notification when the time is up. Nothing about the break leaves the device; the app only counts how many you completed.

Home-screen widget

The optional widget shows your plant, streak and a Breathe button using a small summary the app keeps on your device. It makes no network requests.

Collection and sharing summary

The app does not transmit personal information to OpenWings Wellness or third parties. It has no advertising, analytics, accounts or cloud service. The bundled assistant model sends no telemetry. App data is excluded from Android cloud backup and device transfer through the app's backup configuration.

Retention and deletion

Your information remains locally until you use Preferences & privacy → Clear data, clear Android app storage, or uninstall the app. Clearing data is permanent and also turns reminders off. Updates normally retain data when installed over the same application. The app does not provide cloud recovery. Share cards are written to a temporary cache folder and removed within an hour.

Access and security

Android's app sandbox protects app storage. Anyone who can access your unlocked device may be able to see your activity within the app, including any notes you write. Keep your device secure. No system can guarantee absolute security.

Not a medical device

OpenWings Daily offers general wellness activities. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. If things feel too heavy, please contact someone you trust or a local support service.
